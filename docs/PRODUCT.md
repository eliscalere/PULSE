# Product

## Register

product

## Users
AEWTTR team members across PM, engineering, cyber, contracts, support, and leadership-adjacent review roles use this workspace during daily program execution. They open a project to understand status, manage tasks, move work through supporting tools, and find the right files, forms, and follow-up actions without hunting across disconnected screens.

## Product Purpose
This product is a shared AEWTTR workspace that organizes projects and the supporting tools around them. Its job is to let the whole team enter through a project, see the relevant workstreams for that project, and quickly act in tracker, checklists, travel, document review, imports, and support flows. Success looks like a project page that feels like a clear operational hub rather than a loose collection of separate apps.

## Brand Personality
Practical, clear, dependable. The interface should feel task-focused and operational, with enough structure that team members can understand what to do next at a glance. It should support fast execution without feeling brittle or confusing.

## Anti-references
This should not feel like a flashy marketing dashboard, a generic AI-generated admin template, or a workflow maze that hides actions behind unclear gestures. It should avoid awkward interaction dependencies such as double-click-only editing, disconnected navigation, and unlabeled file/document context.

## Design Principles
1. Projects are the front door: users should open a project and immediately see the tools, documents, and workflows relevant to that project.
2. Actions should be explicit: editing, expanding, attaching files, and navigating between tools should rely on visible controls rather than hidden gestures.
3. Planning should scale from summary to detail: top-level tasks should stay readable while subtasks carry timelines, ownership, and file context.
4. Cross-app flow should preserve context: related tools should feel connected to the same project instead of behaving like isolated modules.
5. Operational clarity beats decoration: labels, counts, status, and next steps should be easier to scan than the visual chrome around them.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Target WCAG AA contrast and keyboard-accessible interactions for core flows. Important actions should not depend on double-click, hover-only affordances, or ambiguous icon-only cues. Motion should stay minimal and task-oriented, and file/location labels should be explicit enough for mixed-role users across the team.
